# Product Vision

> **Planning document.** This describes intended experience and open questions,
> not a guarantee of current capabilities. For current behavior, read the
> [user guide](../using-reason-room.md) and [validation status](../validation.md).

## Product Intent

Large organizations contain overlapping teams, systems, accounts, services,
delivery paths, controls, and responsibilities. Their relationships often cross
organizational boundaries. Documents and diagrams do not make these
relationships easy to understand.

This product gives a person one navigable space to understand those
relationships. It helps them start with broad structure, move into detail that
explains it, and communicate the result without rebuilding a diagram for every
question.

## Intended Outcome

A person can use the canvas to answer questions such as:

- Who is involved in this outcome or environment?
- What sits within this organizational or operational boundary?
- Which paths, dependencies, and responsibilities connect these areas?
- What changes when I look from a different perspective?
- Where do several groups share responsibility for one process?

The product should make these questions easier to explore than a collection of
static diagrams, documents, and disconnected records.

## Experience Principles

- **Direct before formal.** Let a person act on the canvas before asking for
  detailed data.
- **Small inputs.** Creation starts with a name and an optional short summary.
  More detail is available when it has a clear purpose.
- **Spatial meaning.** Arrangement, containment, and proximity help a person
  read the organization. Visual placement does not prove a business fact.
- **Context at every scale.** Zooming and panning must keep people oriented.
- **Detail by need.** Show the detail needed for a question, not every known
  fact at once.
- **Flexible language.** Organizations can use their own labels and concepts.
  The product does not require a prescribed entity taxonomy.
- **Clear meaning.** Connections and boundaries must communicate their meaning
  or uncertainty. A gesture alone must not infer meaning.
- **Low operational burden.** Avoid dense menus, repeated forms, and setup work
  that does not help a person understand the system.

## Product Boundaries for Discovery

- Focus first on individual and small-group sensemaking around complex
  organizational systems.
- Keep the scope open across organization types and domains.
- Do not assume one industry, delivery process, cloud provider, or internal
  terminology defines the product.
- Do not make implementation architecture a product decision before discovery
  identifies a user need.

## Open Questions

- Which visual cues distinguish containment, ownership, dependency,
  participation, and context without clutter?
- How can a person express a relationship quickly and keep it understandable
  later?
- Which details belong in the canvas, and which should appear only on request?
- When should a lens change visibility, grouping, emphasis, or all three?
- How can the product support changing organizational language without losing
  clarity in existing maps?
