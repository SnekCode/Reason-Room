# Discovery Plan

## Aim

Reduce product risk through small, realistic discovery cycles. Each cycle tests
whether people can understand and edit a complex organizational map with less
effort and confusion.

## Phase 1: Questions and Examples

Collect a limited set of representative situations. Include cross-team work,
shared environments, delivery paths, and control points. Write the questions a
person needs the map to answer at broad, medium, and close detail.

**Evidence to seek:** people agree on the questions and can explain what they
need to see to answer each one.

## Phase 2: Composition Prototype

Start this phase only with a representative question and example collected in
Phase 1.

Test direct creation, naming, short summaries, connections, movement, and
nesting. Compare boundary and connection treatments. Observe where people need
meaning made explicit.

**Evidence to seek:** people can build and revise a useful map without a
form-heavy workflow or repeated guidance.

## Phase 3: Scale and Lens Prototype

Test broad-to-close navigation on the same example. Try a small number of
question-led lenses. Measure whether people retain orientation and can recover
context after focused inspection.

**Evidence to seek:** people can find relevant detail and explain how it fits
the wider system.

## Phase 4: Scenario Walkthroughs

Ask people to trace a dependency, explain shared responsibility, locate a
delivery path, and discuss a proposed change. Use failures to refine visual
language and interaction. Do not add form fields by default.

**Evidence to seek:** people can use the canvas to reason about an unfamiliar
or changing part of the organization.

## Phase 5: Product Decisions

Turn repeated evidence into product decisions. Define only the concepts,
constraints, and technical capabilities required to support the validated
experience. Keep uncertain items as open questions.

**Evidence to seek:** each proposed commitment traces back to an observed user
need or a clear product constraint.

## Review Cadence

After each phase, record what people could do, where they hesitated, and which
assumptions changed. Update [Product Vision](vision.md), [Core Interaction Model](core-interaction-model.md), and [Lenses and Semantic Zoom](lenses-and-semantic-zoom.md) before defining later delivery work.

For the first boundary-composition session, use the
[Boundary Discovery Protocol](boundary-discovery-protocol.md). It keeps the
session focused on observed meaning before a data-model or interface decision.
